# Governance of an Internal Audit Function

**Scope warning: most readers do not need this file.**

This covers running an internal audit **function** inside an organization large
enough to have one: a charter, a standing plan, dedicated staff, a reporting
line to a board or audit committee, and a quality program. If you are an owner
reviewing your own books, or performing a single engagement, **skip this
file.** Nothing in it makes a one-person review better, and applying it to one
produces ceremony instead of work.

Read it when the question is "how should our internal audit function be set
up," or when you are evaluating someone else's function.

---

## A note on the standards vocabulary

The professional framework for internal auditing is published by the Institute
of Internal Auditors. **It was restructured recently, and older material uses
vocabulary that no longer exists.**

**Verified against theiia.org on 19 September 2026:** the **Global Internal
Audit Standards** were released 9 January 2024 and became **effective 9 January
2025**, superseding the 2017 International Professional Practices Framework.
The five separately named mandatory elements of the old framework (the Mission
of Internal Audit, the Definition of Internal Auditing, the Core Principles,
the Code of Ethics, and the Standards) **no longer exist as separate
entities.** Their content was incorporated into the Standards themselves.

Practical consequences:

- Material written before 2024, including a great deal of what is currently
  published online, uses the superseded structure. It is not worthless, but its
  vocabulary is a generation behind.
- **Do not quote a standard number from memory or from a secondary source.**
  Pull the current text from the IIA directly.
- Conformance claims ("this function conforms with the Standards") are
  meaningful only against the current version, and only where an assessment
  actually supports the claim.

The principles below are durable. The labels are not, so verify labels before
putting them in a document.

## The charter

The founding document. It is approved by the board or audit committee, not by
management, and that distinction is the whole point.

It states:

- **Purpose.** Why the function exists.
- **Authority.** Access to records, people, and property. Without unrestricted
  access stated in writing, the function will be refused access at exactly the
  moment access matters.
- **Responsibility.** The scope of work, and explicitly what is excluded.
- **Reporting lines.** Functionally to the board or audit committee;
  administratively to management. **If the function reports only to the
  people it audits, it is not independent, whatever the charter says.**
- **Independence and objectivity**, including how conflicts are handled and how
  consulting work is kept from compromising assurance work.

Review it periodically and have the board re-approve it.

## The manual

The charter says what the function is for. The manual says how the work gets
done: engagement procedures, workpaper standards, report formats,
confidentiality handling, communication protocols, and how results are
monitored. It is what makes the output consistent across people, and it is the
basis for assessing performance.

## Role in risk management, and the roles that are off limits

A function's involvement in risk management sits somewhere on a continuum, and
where it sits is a decision for senior management and the board, recorded in
the charter:

1. No role
2. Auditing the risk management process as part of the plan
3. Providing insight and historical data from audit findings
4. Active support: sitting on committees, monitoring, status reporting
5. Managing and coordinating the risk management process

**Positions 4 and 5 buy involvement at the cost of independence**, and a
function at position 5 cannot credibly assure the process it runs.

### What internal audit must never take on

This list is the practical core of independence, and it is violated constantly
by well-meaning functions that want to be helpful:

- **Setting risk appetite.** That is the board's.
- **Imposing risk management processes.** Recommend, do not mandate.
- **Giving management assurance on risks.** Management asserts; audit tests
  the assertion.
- **Deciding risk responses.** Advise on options. Do not choose.
- **Implementing responses on management's behalf.** The moment you build the
  control, you are auditing your own work next cycle.
- **Being accountable for risk management.** Accountability sits with
  management, permanently.

**The formulation worth memorizing: advise and challenge, do not decide.**

Where the function does consulting work as well as assurance, put safeguards in
writing: who does which, how long before someone may audit an area they
advised on, and disclosure in the report when the two overlap.

## What assurance on risk management actually means

The function forms a view on whether risk management is effective. The criteria:

- Organizational objectives support and align with the mission
- Significant risks are identified and assessed
- Responses are selected that align the risk with the stated appetite
- Relevant risk information is captured and communicated promptly enough for
  people to act on it

Evidence for that view comes from examining:

- **Standing.** Does risk management have real management support and real
  resources? Is it part of decisions, or a document produced annually?
- **Framework and criteria.** Appropriate to this organization's size,
  structure, and environment, rather than borrowed from a much larger one.
- **Capability.** Are criteria communicated, are people trained, are they held
  accountable for their part?
- **Communication.** Does information flow back, and outward to external
  stakeholders where required?
- **Monitoring and reporting.** Regular reporting to senior management and the
  board, and measurable enough to improve.
- **Consistency.** Applied the same way across the organization.
- **Responsiveness.** Reassessed when the environment changes, at a frequency
  that matches how fast it changes.

## Competence

The function as a whole must hold the knowledge it needs, or obtain it. That
is a statement about the **team**, not about each person.

- Not everyone needs to be an accountant. A capable function may include
  engineers, IT specialists, legal knowledge, and operational expertise.
- Everyone needs **enough** knowledge of fraud risk and of information
  technology risk and controls to know when to escalate. Not expertise;
  enough to recognize what they are looking at.
- Certification indicates proficiency and does not substitute for relevant
  experience.
- **Name the gap rather than covering it.** An engagement that needs expertise
  the team does not have gets co-sourced, outsourced, or descoped, and the
  report says which.

## Administration

Whoever leads the function owns:

- **Planning.** The risk-based plan, resource needs, and competency gaps. And
  communicating **what will not get done** under the resources available. An
  unstated resource limit becomes an unexplained coverage gap.
- **Organizing.** Assignments, scheduling, engagement procedures, contracting
  external providers, quality and budget monitoring.
- **Directing.** Communication inside and outside the organization, hiring,
  performance management, development.
- **Controlling.** That procedures are followed, objectives met, budgets
  monitored, and stakeholders served.

And periodic reporting to senior management and the board on the function's own
**purpose, authority, responsibility, and performance against its plan**, plus
significant risk exposures, control issues including fraud risk, and governance
issues.

## Quality assurance and improvement

A function is expected to run a program that evaluates its own conformance and
effectiveness and identifies improvement. **This applies even where the
function is fully outsourced**, and the provider's own firm-wide program does
not discharge the organization's obligation.

**Internal assessments** have two parts:

- **Ongoing.** Routine supervision, review of work in progress, and performance
  metrics: plan completion, cycle time, recommendations accepted, stakeholder
  satisfaction.
- **Periodic self-assessment.** A scheduled look at whether the right work is
  being done and whether procedures should change. Covers conformance with the
  standards and ethics requirements, adequacy of the charter and procedures,
  contribution to governance and risk management, and whether the function adds
  value.

**External assessments** are conducted by a qualified, independent assessor or
team: independent meaning no real or apparent conflict, and not part of or
controlled by the organization. Acceptable forms include a full external
assessment, a self-assessment with independent validation, or a peer review
team drawn from several different organizations. Competence is assessed at the
**team** level, not per person.

An external report should give an opinion on conformance based on a structured
rating process, evaluate practices against what is observed and available
elsewhere, and make recommendations.

**Verify the required frequency of external assessment against the current
standard.** It is a specific interval, it is stated in the Standards, and it is
exactly the kind of figure that should not be quoted from a secondary source.

Results go to the stakeholders: findings, planned remedial action, and
follow-up confirming the action happened. **A quality program whose results are
never reported is not a quality program.**

## The recurring failure worth naming

The most common defect in a real internal audit function is not technical. It
is that **findings are reported and nothing happens**, cycle after cycle,
because the function reports to the people whose area the finding concerns and
has no route past them.

The structural answers are a reporting line to the board, tracking open
findings across cycles with dates and owners, and reporting the **status of
prior recommendations** as a standing agenda item. A repeat finding is more
important than a new one, because it says the channel is broken rather than
that a control is.
